These estimates treat every ride as identical, as if crime were spread evenly across every hour, line and station in the system. That is obviously untrue, since crime clusters at particular stations, times and situations, so any one rider's real risk could be meaningfully higher or lower than what is shown here.

Note: Risk is estimated with a Poisson model from the systemwide rate of reported crime per million rides, which includes incidents involving transit workers and others who were not riding. The average rider figure divides roughly 1.28 billion annual rides by the city's 8.585 million residents, which tourists and commuters from outside the city push somewhat higher, and the commuter presets assume a round trip on each office day across 50 working weeks.

Source: NYPD transit crime complaints and MTA subway ridership, January through September 2026. Rates will change as crime on the subway rises or falls.
